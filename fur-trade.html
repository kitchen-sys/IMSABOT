Bienvenue, Coureur des Bois

The year is 1735. You are a French fur trader venturing into the rich Ohio Territory, known to the French as La Belle Rivière (the Beautiful River). Here, mighty rivers flow with opportunity, and powerful Native American nations control vast hunting grounds.

Your Journey:

  • Build trust with Native American tribes: Shawnee, Miami, Delaware, and Huron
  • Trade European goods for valuable furs
  • Navigate the growing rivalry between France and Britain
  • Make choices that will shape the path to war
  • Honor the land and its people while seeking fortune

Your choices will determine not only your success as a trader, but also the alliances that will shape the coming conflict known as the French and Indian War.

Historical Vocabulary

Key terms from the French colonial period and fur trade era

Coureur des Bois

French "runner of the woods" - independent fur traders who worked directly with Native Americans, often living among them.

Voyageur

Licensed French-Canadian canoemen who transported furs and trade goods through wilderness waterways.

La Belle Rivière

French name for the Ohio River, meaning "the Beautiful River," central to the fur trade network.

Nouvelle-France

New France - the French colonial empire in North America, stretching from Canada to Louisiana.

Shawnee

Powerful Algonquian-speaking tribe in the Ohio Valley, known for their diplomatic skill and military prowess.

Miami

Algonquian tribe controlling key portages and trade routes in the western Ohio Territory and Great Lakes region.

Delaware (Lenape)

Eastern Algonquian people pushed westward by British colonization, seeking new alliances in Ohio.

Huron (Wendat)

Long-standing French allies from the Great Lakes, experienced traders and middlemen in the fur trade.

Beaver Pelt

Most valuable fur in the trade, used for making felt hats in Europe. Nearly caused beaver extinction in some regions.

Trade Goods

European items traded for furs: metal tools, guns, cloth, beads, kettles, and blankets.

Fort Duquesne

French fort built at the Forks of the Ohio (modern Pittsburgh), strategic point controlling river access.

Ohio Company

British land speculation company granted 200,000 acres in Ohio, threatening French claims and Native lands.

Iroquois Confederacy

Powerful alliance of six nations holding nominal claims to Ohio, playing French and British against each other.

Calumet

Sacred ceremonial pipe used in Native American diplomacy and peace negotiations.

Wampum

Shell beads used as currency, records, and diplomatic gifts in Native American and colonial trade.

Portage

Carrying canoes and goods overland between waterways - critical points controlled by Native tribes.

French and Indian War

North American theater of the Seven Years' War (1754-1763) between Britain and France for continental control.

Fort Necessity

Hastily built British fort where young George Washington surrendered to French forces in July 1754.

Troupes de la Marine

French colonial regular troops who garrisoned forts and fought alongside Native allies.

Half King

Title for Tanacharison, Seneca leader who attempted to maintain Iroquois influence in Ohio during colonial conflicts.

Métis

People of mixed French and Native American ancestry, often serving as cultural intermediaries and traders.

Rendezvous

Annual gathering where traders, trappers, and Native peoples met to exchange furs, goods, and news.

Albany Plan

1754 proposal by Benjamin Franklin for colonial unity, partly in response to French expansion in Ohio.

Treaty of Logstown

1752 agreement where some Ohio tribes granted British trading rights, causing division and resentment.
